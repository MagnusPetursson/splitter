4 Joints

Select a cut face in the 3D view (Cuts) or below to see its joint map.
How joints are placed
  1. Each cut face is sliced exactly and split into contact patches, one per pair of touching parts.
  2. A distance field (Euclidean distance transform) of each patch gives, for every point, the largest circle that fits there: its ridge is the medial axis, its maximum the largest inscribed circle, and its typical ridge value the wall thickness.
  3. A point joint is only allowed where the field is ≥ joint radius + clearance + minimum wall, so it can never break through a visible surface or reach into a third part.
  4. Depth is checked by ray casting into both parts (centre and 8 rim rays, also against the parts' other cut faces); pins get shorter or move if the material is too shallow.
  5. Joints are spread by farthest-point sampling (≥ 2 per patch for anti-rotation, more on large faces).
  6. Thin shells (walls too thin for pins) get a seam joint along the wall instead: finger joints follow the wall around ring-shaped sections; half laps and tongues are also available.
  7. Integrated pegs and seams go on the part that does not print on that face, so the face stays flat on the bed. Horizontal holes get teardrop roofs, horizontal pegs a flat underside.

5 Build & export

Joint test coupon (< 30 min)

Two bars: pegs of the chosen diameter, sockets with six clearance steps (dimple count = step). Print with the same printer, nozzle and filament, find the loosest step that still holds, and enter it as your fit.

print-splitter

Workflow: 1 load a model → 2 choose the printer → 3 auto split (move, tilt, add or remove cuts as you like) → 4 review and edit joints on each cut face → 5 build the solids in your browser and export STL/3MF plates and an assembly sheet.

Shortcuts: 1–4 views · Del delete selected cut/joint · Esc deselect · A add-joint mode.

Geometry: the Go planner (compiled to WebAssembly, running in your browser) repairs the mesh, plans cuts, analyses each cut face and places joints. The Manifold kernel builds the parts in a second Web Worker. Your model never leaves your computer, so every part is guaranteed watertight and manifold. Physical fit is untested until printed — print the coupon first.
